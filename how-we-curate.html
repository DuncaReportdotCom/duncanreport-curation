How We Curate

We want you informed on the facts. Most major news outlets have settled into advocacy journalism and clickbait headlines; it's our job to keep you informed of the ground truth, as best we're able — and then let you decide.

DuncanReport.com exists to show you the news across the political spectrum — not filtered through a single lens — so you can see the whole picture and make up your own mind.

The 50/50 policy

Most news sites, knowingly or not, lean one direction. We do the opposite on purpose. Our sources are balanced 50/50 between left-leaning and right-leaning outlets, and we hold that balance deliberately — in the row of sources at the top of every page, and in the mix of stories we feature. When the left and the right are given equal footing, the slant tends to cancel out, and what's left is closer to the plain facts. That balance isn't an afterthought; it's the entire point of the site.

How stories are chosen

We are constantly scanning the web for the best and most interesting stories from across the spectrum, surfacing the ones drawing the most coverage. Related coverage is grouped together so a developing story stays visible, and every headline links straight to the original article on the publisher's own site — we send you to the source, we don't republish it. Stories rotate through a rolling multi-day window so nothing important disappears overnight.

Editorials, side by side

For major stories we also link opinion and editorials from across the spectrum, labeled by the outlet that wrote them. Reading the case the left makes and the case the right makes, right next to each other, is one of the fastest ways to understand an argument — and to judge it for yourself.

What we won't do

We don't editorialize inside the news. We don't bury stories because they're inconvenient to one side or the other. We don't rewrite, paywall, or claim other people's reporting as our own. And we don't tell you what to think — the balanced presentation is a tool, and the conclusions are yours.

Corrections and feedback

If we've mislabeled a source, linked a broken page, or gotten something wrong, tell us — see the Contact page. We read every message.
